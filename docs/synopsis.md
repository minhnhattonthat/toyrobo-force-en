# Synopsis - Toy Robo Force

Full spoilers. Based on s00–s23, the glossary, and the English cast list. Dialogue IDs identify text records, not a single uninterrupted playthrough: choices, retries, and optional conversations coexist. In particular, s23 contains the finale followed by supplementary dialogue for earlier chapters.

## Plot by section

**s00 — dlg:0000–0022.** Chief Sumita tests whether a Polisteka can understand a Teka Revolt. It understands robots and disorder separately, but treats robots committing crimes as absurd comedy. Human children are therefore being trained as police officers. Sumita and Mayor Adolf privately discuss the political consequences of a revolt occurring before those children are ready.

**s01 — dlg:0023–0084.** At Apricot Police Academy, Instructor Pauillac drills Yuki, Tomomi, Gilligan, and Blank. Blank is Yuki’s childhood Toyrobo, rebuilt as a Polisteka, but still does domestic chores and nervously stutters. Arrest training establishes the team’s roles, the acknowledgement “Confirm!”, Tomomi’s “Good kids…” scoldings, and Gilligan’s frustration at playing the criminal instead of riding a police motorcycle.

**s02 — dlg:0085–0190.** Gun-chan, a Workteka at Camembert Ranch, goes berserk. Mii-chan reports the destruction without understanding that another Teka is causing it; Polijenne likewise cannot interpret the emergency. Sumita disguises the assignment as “field training.” Yuki recognizes the real danger and authorizes live fire. Afterward, the team reasons that ordinary loss of control should stop a Teka, suggesting outside manipulation.

**s03 — dlg:0191–0350.** A missing kindergarten bus initially looks like an unannounced excursion, but Yuki exposes Polisteka No. 37, Sannana, as its hijacker. Failed investigation approaches give way to Tomomi tracing his signal. Sannana demands ransom for twenty children and bets Emiko that humans will shoot him; if they do, she must serve his elderly master. Blank destroys Sannana and saves the children. Emiko thanks their rescuers but honors her promise. At the Hidden Lodge, the Doctor and Richter discuss their failed ransom scheme; Richter is presented as a human boy whose illness required a mechanical body.

**s04 — dlg:0351–0541.** The villains punish Adolf’s refusal to pay by controlling Apricot’s Teka en masse. Pauillac asks to be restrained before losing control; Blank remains unaffected because of his different construction. With Sumita admitting his lack of practical police experience, Yuki leads the response. Investigation points toward the harbor, although half the fleeing Teka escape by ship. Those left recover, including Pauillac. At the lodge, Emiko now works as a maid, while Richter proposes acquiring a human body—a suggestion the Doctor rejects.

**s05 — dlg:0542–0554.** The human cadets receive their appointments: Tomomi Attenborough and Gilligan Brock become Officers, and Yuki Tachibana becomes Senior Officer because he commands the team. Tomomi resents his higher rank until she receives her controller’s license; Gilligan immediately asks for the motorcycle license that motivated him to join.

**s06 — dlg:0555–0687.** Adolf takes his daughter Riana to Framboise, ostensibly to recover Apricot’s escaped Teka, and requires a four-robot escort. Riana treats Peta dismissively and the police imperiously. Once the destination appears safe, Adolf dismisses the escort; he and Riana are then kidnapped. Emiko restrains Richter’s appetite for violence, but the villains still use the hostages to lure the team into a winery trap. Blank rescues Riana, who promptly falls for him and starts calling him “Blank-sama”; Adolf remains missing.

**s07 — dlg:0688–0776.** Mayor Framboise, an instructor Teka turned politician, negotiates help while Adolf is held on an offshore oil rig and a local revolt develops. Dialogue supports different priorities and responses: helping Framboise first or rescuing Adolf first changes the exchanges and assistance. Blank eventually rescues Adolf, now frightened of Teka, and the team suppresses the city’s revolt. Riana’s devotion to Blank displaces Peta’s hopes; Tomomi jokingly recommends a policewoman’s uniform to attract Blank.

**s08 — dlg:0777–0781.** Traumatized by his kidnapping, Adolf decides that military force is the answer. He orders Sumita and the team to accompany him to militaristic Cassis to borrow an army, over Sumita’s objections.

**s09 — dlg:0782–0972.** General Mordel offers assistance if the Apricot officers retake an arsenal occupied by opponents of militarism. The principal branch is to attack or negotiate. On the attack route, Yuki insists on protecting the opposition’s lives, earns Mordel’s respect, and secures a platoon rather than the suggested battalion. On the negotiation route, the opposition’s spokesperson proves to be a Teka defending a factory from military appropriation; the team fights Cassis forces to protect it and loses the promised loan. Elsewhere, Richter struggles to control Soldiers, whose design differs from civilian Teka.

**s10 — dlg:0973–1151.** Colonel Erwin’s coup captures Mordel. The team searches for Sumita through guarded streets, obtaining a headquarters pass and local police authentication. Soldiers then disarm and begin volunteer work: the coup is intended to bring peace. Georg publicly identifies himself and exploits the army’s disarmament to unleash civilian Teka. Yuki reluctantly rescues Mordel so the army can suppress the revolt, while promising to prove that armies should be unnecessary. Richter suggests using the bomber Prometheus, but Emiko persuades him to desist.

**s11 — dlg:1152–1256.** Repeated small revolts consume the team’s days. Depending on the Cassis outcome, borrowed Soldiers can handle assignments, but they destroy the targets completely, leaving neither salvage nor much room for compassion. The human officers complain about their repetitive life and enjoy food-related diversions. Yuki proposes visiting Lingonberry Capital, outwardly to eat sushi, while recognizing that the continuing disturbances may conceal another operation.

**s12 — dlg:1257–1362.** Yuki argues that small revolts are pinning the team down while the enemy prepares elsewhere. In Lingonberry, impostors have already passed themselves off as the visiting Apricot officers, so the real team is arrested. Yuki and Tomomi share a cell; Gilligan is taken away after resisting. Waiting and feigning illness fail, and the local Polisteka reveal that their respect-for-life program has been removed. Yuki orders Blank to escape, contact Sumita, and rescue them. Blank frees Yuki and Tomomi; Gilligan remains with the special police.

**s13 — dlg:1363–1559.** The fugitives evade pursuit through streets, buildings, and sewers. Supplementary records in s23 supply the discovery of Jiro, Sumita’s rescue Polisteka, destroyed beyond repair; an apparent Jiro in this section is an impostor exposed through a deliberately nonexistent password. The team learns that a Stealth Shield is needed to penetrate the special police’s defenses, raids Georg’s abandoned institute beyond Kanpachi Dam, and rescues Gilligan. Georg then plans to attack Yuki indirectly, treating Blank as a dangerous weapon rather than an easy target.

**s14 — dlg:1560–1569.** Back in Apricot, Adolf asks the Teka crime team to find Peta. Riana reveals that Peta announced his intention to become a police officer in order to compete with Blank. Adolf’s supposedly heartfelt appeal repeats his earlier fake-crying routine.

**s15 — dlg:1570–1794.** Peta follows disastrously literal advice: if a machine body can become a Polisteka, he will become mechanical; if mechanization requires serious injury, he will get injured. The team rescues him from unfamiliar Polijenne, linking the trouble to Marmalade. Riana supplies an introduction in exchange for a date with Blank. Secretary Jane offers Tomomi the police chief’s position, apparently buying her allegiance. A coded message about Robin Hood proposing seven times to Marian directs Blank to capture seven Polijenne in Sherwood Forest. There, Father Tuck, Hanna’s former tutor, resolves to organize citizens against the city’s discriminatory rule.

**s16 — dlg:1795–1913.** Jane overrides Tomomi’s orders to protect demonstrators. Georg broadcasts threats, and a slowly moving goldfish-bowl surface prompts an investigation into his hiding place: earthquake and airship theories fail; the sea is correct. The villains realize their plans are leaking and launch another revolt to cover their escape. After the team suppresses it, the citizens’ revolution proceeds. Yuki reveals that Tomomi’s defection was a planned infiltration, Hanna is already dead, and Jane’s memories were rewritten. Speaking through Jane, Georg says Hanna wanted to erase Jane and occupy her body. He asks that Jane receive proper repairs.

**s17 — dlg:1914–1944.** Marmalade begins reforming its police, Jane is sent for conversion into an ordinary Marketeka, and Tomomi discovers that the local Polisteka remain devoted to her. The team attacks Georg’s ship but finds it abandoned. Yuki concludes that pursuing the villains from outside is insufficient: someone inside must help them.

**s18 — dlg:1945–1966.** Georg interrupts routine police radio traffic to ask about missing teacher Emiko. He calls her family and his grandson’s girlfriend, offering to halve the revolts if she is returned. Yuki does not have her, but recognizes a promising lead.

**s19 — dlg:1967–2237.** The team follows Emiko from Sannana’s grave toward Lingonberry, where police detained and then released her despite her expressed wish to die. Many possible destinations are false leads; she is sheltering at Father Tuck’s church. Gilligan promises protection and fiercely rejects using her as a bargaining chip; Yuki subsequently refuses Sumita’s proposed trade. Emiko falls for Gilligan and reveals an island base concealing a bomber. She accompanies the team to Blackberry Island. The audience learns that Richter can still share her perceptions, and that the island’s helpful Domesteka is lying for Georg in exchange for software upgrades.

**s20 — dlg:2238–2294.** The team searches the volcanic base’s entrances, with repeated snake and lizard scares undercutting Yuki’s composure. They find an accessible entrance from the sea, while Yuki prepares a separate escape route through the front shutter. Blank reaches the inner chamber and finds neither people nor bomber. Georg announces that Emiko’s departure and the island lead were part of his trap.

**s21 — dlg:2295–2309.** Yuki springs his own precaution: Gilligan detonates the prepared charge and opens the front shutter. Blank escapes through the new exit. Even the combat-failure dialogue allows him to run out alive. The team plans maintenance and a return to Apricot.

**s22 — dlg:2310–2396.** Georg decides that the team’s partnership with Teka already vindicates his cause and wants to surrender; Richter instead pursues victory and revenge against Gilligan. Yuki identifies transmitters in Emiko’s replacement eyes. Richter attacks with the carrier Argo, which Blank captures, but Prometheus is absent. A gunshot and overheard dialogue make Richter believe Emiko has shot out her eyes and is being taken to Marmalade. Meanwhile, Georg visits Father Tuck, confesses his despair, and resolves to confront Richter.

**s23 — dlg:2397–2926.** The finale proper, dlg:2397–2558, reveals the eye injury as a staged lure. Georg tries to obtain Richter’s surrender, then exposes his human childhood and family memories as fabrications: Richter is a robot Georg created as a companion. Richter escapes with Prometheus and threatens Apricot with catastrophic bombs. Cassis Soldiers cannot shoot down their own aircraft, leaving Blank to stop it with Georg’s help. Richter is finally identified specifically as a Toyrobo. Georg and Emiko leave to recover and potentially rebuild him; the official account says all three disappeared with the bomber. The following spring, Riana and Peta are cadets, Gilligan dates Emiko, and Blank still hides from Riana. **dlg:2559–2926 are supplementary earlier-scene, optional, and postgame records**, including Jiro’s discovery, Riana’s weapon donation, Tomomi’s family background, and a probable encounter with the rebuilt Jane.

## Routes and interpretation limits

- **Cassis is the substantial moral branch:** attack the opposition with strict protection orders, or attempt negotiation and defend it against the army. Borrowed-Soldier availability and later dialogue reflect the outcome (dlg:0862–0961, 1152–1156).
- **Framboise allows competing priorities:** Adolf’s rescue versus local suppression, with corresponding aid and reactions (dlg:0703–0722, 2635–2639). Do not describe every response as one fixed sequence.
- **Investigation branches usually converge:** bus-search methods, escaped Teka destinations, Cassis patrol timing, Lingonberry escape paths, Robin Hood decoding, the goldfish-bowl deduction, and Emiko’s whereabouts include wrong answers and loops.
- **Optional discoveries matter to characterization:** Riana buys Blank an expensive weapon; the Framboise Bankteka protects its creator; Tomomi’s grandfather sends domestic help; Jiro’s remains explain the impostor confrontation.
- The dialogue establishes these alternatives, but does not by itself prove every flag, reward prerequisite, or reachable combination. No separate final ending is demonstrated by these inputs.

## Who is who

- **Yuki Tachibana — human boy; cadet, then Senior Officer.** Apricot’s commander and detective; Blank’s childhood companion and former owner. Friendly and playful, but increasingly firm about protecting citizens and treating Teka as persons.
- **Tomomi Attenborough — human girl; cadet, then Officer and licensed controller.** Sharp, capable teammate; briefly Marmalade’s chief as an undercover operation. Her grandfather and a mansion are mentioned at dlg:2861, suggesting an affluent background. Romantic implications with Yuki remain teasing, not a confirmed relationship.
- **Gilligan Brock — human boy; cadet, then Officer.** Driver, equipment enthusiast, aspiring motorcycle officer, and temporary controller during Tomomi’s absence. Protective of children and attracted to teacher Teka; later Emiko’s confirmed date.
- **Blank — Toyrobo rebuilt as a Polisteka; Officer in the credits.** Yuki’s longtime playmate and the team’s combat operative. His origins protect him from the early occupational-Teka takeover. Courteous, nervous, domestic, and loyal—not merely a cool fighting machine. Riana pursues him; he avoids her.
- **Kozo Sumita — human; Apricot Police Chief.** Bureaucratic superior preoccupied with his pension, but increasingly protective of his officers. Initially admits having no practical police experience. Participates in the final official account that conceals the fugitives’ departure.
- **Adolf Francisca — human; Mayor of Apricot and Riana’s father.** Stingy, vain, election-conscious, and easily frightened; favors Peta as Riana’s partner. Nevertheless refuses to abandon Apricot during the bomber crisis.
- **Riana Francisca — human girl; mayor’s daughter, later cadet.** Proud and forceful; rejects Peta’s assumption that he is her boyfriend, idolizes Blank after her rescue, and uses money and influence to help him. Her concern for Peta occasionally escapes her denials.
- **Peta Kawamoto — human boy; Riana’s self-styled boyfriend, later cadet.** Attempts to rival Blank through police work and even mechanization. His injury scheme fails; the epilogue gives him a constructive path into police training. Do not present Riana’s affection as settled reciprocation.
- **Pauillac — instructor Teka; Apricot Police Academy instructor.** Trains the four leads, trusts them beyond its own conceptual limitations, and briefly becomes controlled. Gender is unspecified: follow the glossary’s “the Instructor”/“it,” without he/she or sir/ma’am.
- **Mayor Framboise — instructor Teka; mayor of Framboise.** Former university politics teacher who took office because the students were inadequate. Negotiates shrewdly and still awards points like an instructor. Gender unspecified; an isolated current-English “that man” is not reliable evidence.
- **General Mordel — human; Cassis military commander.** Authoritarian opponent and reluctant ally of Yuki. Respects Yuki’s courage, survives a pacifist coup, and ultimately cannot help shoot down Cassis’s stolen bomber.
- **Colonel Erwin — Cassis officer and leader of the pacifist coup.** Orders Mordel’s arrest and disarmament. Mentioned rather than directly speaking; apparently human, but these excerpts do not explicitly establish his biological status.
- **Dr. Georg Wentz / Doctor — human engineer.** Teka Revolt organizer, Richter’s creator, and the Framboise institute director’s teacher. Wants humans to recognize Teka as life, yet manipulates those same Teka. Eventually helps stop Richter and leaves to recover him.
- **Richter / Boy with a Machine Face — Toyrobo.** Initially claims to be Georg’s human grandson in a mechanical body; both ancestry and childhood are fabricated. Controls Teka, later military machines, and possessively calls Emiko his sweetheart. His jealousy of Gilligan overtakes Georg’s political purpose.
- **Emiko — female teacher Teka, ’97 Elise type.** Maiko’s colleague at Sazanka Kindergarten. Sannana’s bet takes her to Georg; she tries to restrain Richter, flees, and becomes an unwitting surveillance device. Falls for Gilligan, yet retains compassion for Richter and offers to educate him after repair.
- **Maiko — female teacher Teka, the same model as Emiko.** Reports the missing bus, worries about the children and Emiko, and attracts Gilligan’s early flirtation.
- **Sannana — Polisteka No. 37.** Controlled bus hijacker serving the Doctor; destroys Emiko’s confidence in uncomplicated human–Teka friendship through their wager. Destroyed during the rescue, later mourned by Emiko.
- **Jiro — Polisteka No. 26.** Sent by Sumita to rescue the team in Lingonberry; destroyed, with his electronic brain ruined in the sewer. A separate **impostor using Jiro’s identity** tries to lure the team away; it is presented as a Polisteka, but its precise underlying identity is not established.
- **Hanna — human; deceased Mayor of Marmalade, of noble birth.** Father Tuck’s former pupil and Riana’s acquaintance. Georg says she sought to overwrite Jane with her own memories after learning she was terminally ill. She is not secretly living inside Jane.
- **Jane — female secretary Teka, later converted into a Marketeka.** Believes she still serves Hanna because her memories were rewritten; used as Georg’s surveillance and speaking channel. The grateful hotel worker at dlg:2916–2921 is strongly implied to be Jane, without an explicit admission.
- **Father Tuck — instructor Teka turned priest.** Hanna’s former tutor; organizes resistance to Marmalade’s oppressive government, shelters Emiko, and challenges Georg’s despair. Gender unspecified despite the title “Father.”
- **Mii-chan — Domesteka at Camembert Ranch.** Concerned for Gun-chan, calls for help, and later offers the ranch’s ice cream.
- **Gun-chan — Workteka at Camembert Ranch.** The first shown berserk Teka; Mii-chan tends its battery needs.
- **Mei — secretary Teka serving Adolf.** Named when the mayor notices the mass disappearance (dlg:0384); not Jane.
- **Niiko — Polijenne No. 25.** Known to Tomomi during her Marmalade appointment; no longer recognizes her after reeducation (dlg:2114–2117).
- **Hiro-kun and Kumi-chan — kindergarten children, apparently human.** Greeted by Emiko when boarding the bus (dlg:0192); no further individual characterization.
- **Ikeyama — Yuki’s favorite baseball player.** A named offscreen reference in the false-password test (dlg:1408–1411); no additional identification is established here.
- **Robin Hood and Marian — legendary/code references, not additional local cast members.** In Tomomi’s message, Robin corresponds to Blank, Marian to Polijenne, and seven proposals to seven captures in Sherwood Forest. Gilligan subsequently uses “Marian” as a Polijenne nickname.
- **Adolf-kun, Uncle Mordel, Hanna-sensei, and Sumita-kun — invented people in Richter’s memories.** Their names were borrowed from existing public figures; they are not evidence that those figures actually shared his childhood.
- **Recurring unnamed Teka** include the Framboise Bankteka, whose creator/director embezzled funds; the Lingonberry Workteka, a profit-minded but useful helper; Cassis’s antiwar factory owner; and Blackberry’s bribed Domesteka. Their shared robot-type labels do not make them one individual.

## Relationship changes and late reveals

- **Blank’s origin is established early:** dlg:0040 and 0059, reiterated at 0129–0130. His immunity is explained at 0397–0398; Sumita’s recognition of him as a fully legitimate Polisteka culminates at 2540.
- **Emiko joins Georg through obligation:** the wager is dlg:0292–0296, her departure 0337, and service 0537. Her defense of the rescuers at 0604–0606 prevents reading this as simple ideological conversion.
- **Riana’s attraction begins with her rescue:** dlg:0680–0686. Her rejection of Peta is explicit in supplementary dlg:2625–2630 and 2664–2665. Both become cadets at 2545–2548.
- **Yuki and Mordel develop respect without agreement:** dlg:0899–0911 or the confrontation route, then 1131–1138. Yuki finally asks for military help at 2464–2467; Mordel’s refusal is a programming limitation, not revenge.
- **The Doctor receives his name at dlg:1100.** “Doctor” and “Dr. Georg” are one speaker; Wentz comes from the credits.
- **Jiro’s death and replacement:** the physical discovery is dlg:2807–2810, used during s13; the impostor confrontation is 1401–1418. Numerical ID order must not obscure this dependency.
- **Tomomi never truly defects:** apparent acceptance at dlg:1694–1697; the planned infiltration is disclosed at 1891–1894 and her authorship of the code at 1911.
- **Hanna is already dead; Jane is manipulated:** explicit from dlg:1898. Hanna’s attempted body appropriation is Georg’s account at 1904–1905; its independent verification and exact chronology are not supplied.
- **Emiko chooses Gilligan:** his refusal to trade her is dlg:2149–2161; her love becomes explicit at 2186–2189. Dating is confirmed at 2556. Richter’s “girlfriend” claim is possessive, not proof of reciprocal romance.
- **Emiko’s flight is real but engineered:** Georg admits setting up the trap at dlg:2293; Yuki explains the induced flight and replacement-eye transmitters at 2333–2337. She is an unwitting spy, not a willing betrayer.
- **Georg breaks with Richter:** dlg:2314–2331. **The apparent eye shooting is false:** reported at 2373, explicitly undone at 2406–2409.
- **Richter’s human identity collapses at dlg:2416–2425; Toyrobo is specified at 2519.** His memories were composed, not transferred from a sick child. The bombs are implied to be nuclear by 2454 and 2517, but never named as such.
- **The official deaths are a cover story:** Georg and Emiko depart at dlg:2524–2529; Sumita describes them as lost aboard the bomber at 2531–2533, echoed by `credits:248D44`. Emiko’s later date disproves that account. Richter’s successful reconstruction and Georg’s later whereabouts remain unconfirmed.

## Recurring jokes and callbacks

- **“Confirm!”** is more than radio texture: Yuki withholds it from an order he rejects (dlg:0300–0303). Preserve the same word in ordinary commands, moral disagreements, and the new cadets’ replies.
- **“Good kids…”** lets Tomomi treat towering Blank as a child: manuals and discipline become moral resistance to bad orders (dlg:0069, 0123, 0877–0879).
- **Teka crime as nonsense:** telephone poles laying eggs, misunderstood reports, “humans disguised as Teka,” and the postgame “some kind of game?” all express the same conceptual blind spot. Characters can be articulate without understanding rebellion.
- **Pauillac’s insults become speaker labels:** Yuki is Peanut, then Rice Grain; Tomomi is Barrel; Gilligan is Mantis-Head. Height, waist, and mental age continue the same joke. “Barrel” returns during the surveillance revelation.
- **Food defines all three children:** Yuki’s hamburger steak/curry, Tomomi’s exhaustive cake orders, and Gilligan’s supposedly adult sushi taste. Gilligan is not the sole food-minded character.
- **Pension versus election:** Sumita calculates retirement pay as a hobby; Adolf measures emergencies in electoral consequences. Their refusal to flee during the climax temporarily gives these comic officials dignity.
- **Votes and “deep plans”:** Gilligan proposes abandoning inconvenient work—“I’m for it”; Tomomi and Yuki answer “Against.” Tomomi credits wrong player moves to Yuki’s supposedly profound reasoning. Preserve the reversal when all three vote against leaving (dlg:2878–2881).
- **Romance and police uniforms:** Riana’s “Blank-sama,” Blank’s hiding, Tomomi’s uniform joke, the promised date without any scheduled day off, and Riana’s eventual real cadet uniform form one extended chain.
- **Tools, toys, and persons:** Richter treats armies as toys, while Yuki’s “toy” acts compassionately. “Toyrobo Force,” its joking sequel, and Adolf’s proposed heroic publicity name deliberately echo each other.
- **Small verbal callbacks matter:** spoken “rummage” noises; the antiwar “factory, not arsenal”; Gilligan “seizing evidence”; “rat in a trap”; the island entrance becoming an exit; and “Out of Control” returning for Prometheus’s final fall.
